# S12 · Cylindrical insulation and critical radius

## B · Educational purpose

Add insulation to a small isothermal cylinder. Predict the sign of the first change in heat loss before seeing the curve.

Dragging the outer radius through the maximum exposes two possible thicknesses for one heat-loss target.

Outcome: Bracket on each side of rc, report all thicknesses for a target magnitude; impose any space cap after solving. At zero driving force no unique insulation design. ## C · Mathematical basis

Rcond=ln(ro/ri)/(2πkL); Rconv=1/(2πrohL); Q̇=(Ti−T∞)/(Rcond+Rconv); rc=k/h; T(r)=Ti−Q̇ ln(r/ri)/(2πkL). L=1 m. Fixed inner surface temperature.

## I · Assumptions and applicability

Long cylinder, constant k and h, no ends/radiation/contact. For ri≥rc heat loss decreases monotonically for Ti>T∞. Real h may change with diameter; critical radius is a maximum of heat loss magnitude for fixed driving temperature.

Apply per-output within/approaching/outside/unknown rules. Record assumed facts separately from verified input evidence. Unmodelled physical states or invalid dimensions suppress the corresponding output.

## J · Model alternatives and fidelity

## L · Learning activities

**Explore.** Add insulation to a small isothermal cylinder. Predict the sign of the first change in heat loss before seeing the curve.

**Investigate.** Commit a prediction before changing a control. A learner marks rc as the thickness giving minimum heat loss. At fixed inner temperature it maximises magnitude; thickness there equals rc−ri if positive.

**Challenge and assessment.** Bracket on each side of rc, report all thicknesses for a target magnitude; impose any space cap after solving. At zero driving force no unique insulation design.
