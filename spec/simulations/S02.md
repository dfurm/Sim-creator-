# S02 · Continuity through a contraction

## A · Identification

S02 · specification 0.1.0 · Fluids · primary category C · Introductory · F1 · effort 2/5 · 8–15 min. Maturity: specification drafted. Prototype implementation brief.

## B · Educational purpose

Pinch a pipe while holding throughput fixed. Predict which cross-section has greater mean velocity and whether mass flow changes.

Changing the section without changing parcel throughput makes the diameter-squared relationship visible.

Outcome: Choose a bore to meet a velocity ceiling at fixed delivery; report feasible available bores. Explain why shrinking a real nozzle on a fixed pump may change Q. Prerequisites: No additional block required. Understand / Apply. Lecture demo, tutorial, guided or independent web activity, pre/post-lab. Practice link: EXP01 measured flow and line geometry. Industrial teaching context: Piping, pumping and metering.

## C · Mathematical basis

A=πD²/4; v=Q/A; ṁ=ρQ. Two stations share Q by steady incompressible continuity. Positive axial direction downstream. Density fixed at 1000 kg/m³.

Independent quantities and engineering units appear below. Outputs are derived. Assume SI absolute state variables unless a quantity is explicitly a difference or a gauge reading. Algebraic variants use the listed boundary state and require no time initialisation.

## I · Assumptions and applicability

One-dimensional steady single-phase flow in an impermeable branch-free pipe; acceleration profile and required pressure unknown. For compressible flow use constant ṁ and solve densities from a thermodynamic model.

Apply per-output within/approaching/outside/unknown rules. Record assumed facts separately from verified input evidence. Unmodelled physical states or invalid dimensions suppress the corresponding output.

## L · Learning activities

**Explore.** Pinch a pipe while holding throughput fixed. Predict which cross-section has greater mean velocity and whether mass flow changes.

**Investigate.** Commit a prediction before changing a control. Halve the downstream diameter. A twofold speed prediction misses the square in the area; the correct factor is four.

**Challenge and assessment.** Choose a bore to meet a velocity ceiling at fixed delivery; report feasible available bores. Explain why shrinking a real nozzle on a fixed pump may change Q. Submit a result with units, one rejected interpretation and the condition that would change the answer. Extension: Add a compressible density pair at known states, then a momentum/energy solution if pressures are sought.
