# S11 · Fourier conduction through a composite wall

## A · Identification

S11 · specification 0.1.0 · Heat transfer · primary category C · Introductory · F2 · effort 3/5 · 8–15 min. Maturity: specification drafted. Prototype implementation brief.

## B · Educational purpose

Move a material interface and change its conductivity separately. Thickness changes resistance; the intrinsic conductivity of a homogeneous constant-property material stays fixed.

Changing thickness without changing material separates conductivity from resistance and splits interface drops visibly.

Outcome: Choose minimum L₂ to meet a heat-flux limit within a depth constraint; flag already feasible and impossible cases. Repeat with a worse contact and explain the interface temperatures. Practice link: Proposed instrumented wall specimen; lecture-ready without equipment. Industrial teaching context: Thermal processing and heat exchange.

## C · Mathematical basis

R″=L₁/k₁+Rc″+L₂/k₂; q″=(Th−Tc)/R″; Q̇=Aq″; interface faces Th−q″L₁/k₁ and Tc+q″L₂/k₂. Positive heat flux left to right; isothermal external faces; A=1 m².

## I · Assumptions and applicability

1-D steady conduction with no generation, uniform A, piecewise constant k, negligible edge losses. Single-layer preset removes second layer and its contact. Alternative k(T) requires ∫k(T)dT and matched interface flux; transient case needs ρcp and IC.

Apply per-output within/approaching/outside/unknown rules. Record assumed facts separately from verified input evidence. Unmodelled physical states or invalid dimensions suppress the corresponding output.

## L · Learning activities

**Explore.** Move a material interface and change its conductivity separately. Thickness changes resistance; the intrinsic conductivity of a homogeneous constant-property material stays fixed.

**Investigate.** Commit a prediction before changing a control. Double L₁ at fixed k₁. Its resistance doubles; total resistance rises from .15 to .20 and heat flux falls from 200 to 150 W/m². Ask why the flux did not halve.

**Challenge and assessment.** Choose minimum L₂ to meet a heat-flux limit within a depth constraint; flag already feasible and impossible cases. Repeat with a worse contact and explain the interface temperatures. Submit a result with units, one rejected interpretation and the condition that would change the answer. Extension: Temperature-dependent k, edge heat loss and contact pressure need independent specimen data and boundary temperatures.
