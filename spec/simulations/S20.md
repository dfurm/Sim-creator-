## C · Mathematical basis

For −rA=kCA, constant density: XC=kτ/(1+kτ); XP=1−exp(−kτ); VC=(Q/k)X/(1−X); VP=−(Q/k)ln(1−X). Plot FA0/(−rA)=Q/[k(1−X)] against conversion. τ=V/Q.

## I · Assumptions and applicability

First-order irreversible isothermal reaction, ideal mixing or ideal plug flow. Conversion ranking conditional on rate law; evaluate full integral for inhibition, reversible and variable-density cases. X is calculated in performance mode, V calculated in design.

Apply per-output within/approaching/outside/unknown rules. Record assumed facts separately from verified input evidence. Unmodelled physical states or invalid dimensions suppress the corresponding output.

## J · Model alternatives and fidelity

Integrate measured kinetics using independent batch data; add axial dispersion, heat transfer and phase variation only when assumptions require them.

Starter cost: direct scalar evaluation per rendered sample. Escalate when the omitted mechanism changes the decision by more than the uncertainty in the comparison, and when the additional data can be identified. See the fidelity and correlation standards for F-tag and evidence scope.

## K · Engineering judgement and design

Document the target, remaining degrees of freedom, active constraints and feasible alternatives. Compare any inverse result by forward substitution; preserve infeasibility and multiple solutions. Cost requires a dated independent basis; use the physical trade-off otherwise.

## L · Learning activities

**Explore.** Place a rectangle and the area under a rate curve on a Levenspiel plot. Predict which ideal reactor needs more volume at equal conversion.

**Investigate.** Commit a prediction before changing a control. For X=.5 compare two reactors on the same rate curve. Exit-rate rectangle uses low exit rate over the entire mixed reactor; area integral samples the falling rate.

**Challenge and assessment.** Given a volume budget choose reactor type or tanks in series; report infeasible targets or multiple viable configurations.
