# S17 · Diffusive flux and transient concentration

## B · Educational purpose

Distinguish a steady gradient through a membrane from a front entering an initially uniform medium. Doubling thickness and doubling time have different effects.

Switching the boundary-value problem changes the shape of the entire profile, from a line to a moving diffusive front.

Outcome: Choose steady barrier thickness to limit flux, then explain why using that relation for breakthrough time is unjustified. Prerequisites: S01 Rate, gradient and accumulated volume, S11 Fourier conduction through a composite wall. Understand / Apply. Lecture demo, tutorial, guided or independent web activity. Practice link: Independent tracer diffusion or membrane coupon demonstration. Industrial teaching context: Diffusion, drying and gas–liquid transfer.

## L · Learning activities

**Explore.** Distinguish a steady gradient through a membrane from a front entering an initially uniform medium. Doubling thickness and doubling time have different effects.

**Investigate.** Commit a prediction before changing a control. At four times t the same concentration ratio moves twice as far into the solid. Interpreting it as four times the distance confuses diffusion with advection.

**Challenge and assessment.** Choose steady barrier thickness to limit flux, then explain why using that relation for breakthrough time is unjustified. Submit a result with units, one rejected interpretation and the condition that would change the answer.
