# Research and benchmark assessment

## What the evidence supports

Undergraduate resources already let students change parameters, observe flows and temperatures, step equilibrium stages and collect virtual data. LearnChemE even distinguishes simulations, self-study and digital experiments. Paying for a second interface to the same calculation is a weak proposition. The testable Furmtech proposition is continuity of evidence across a lesson: ask for a prediction, identify the governing assumptions, save exactly what was calculated, then explain a discrepancy against a recorded physical run. The first sales or teaching pilot should measure whether academics actually use that chain. No market-size or purchasing-intent estimate follows from this review. [LEARN, VIRTUAL, PHET, TRAY]

## Evidence to teaching design

Freeman et al. aggregate undergraduate STEM active-learning evidence; the finding supports using a simulation in an active task, not promising a specific score improvement for this product [ACTIVE]. Adams et al. investigate students using simulations and motivate trials of discoverability and representational clarity [DESIGN]. Mayer and Moreno support segmenting explanation and coordinating relevant words and graphics [LOAD]. Karpicke and Roediger support requiring retrieval after exposure; their experimental context differs from engineering-design assessment [RETRIEVE]. Accordingly, collect an initial prediction, have the student explain a surprising case, then pose a later transfer problem without the first worked solution. Conduct a local pilot before making efficacy claims.

## Validation of the product hypothesis

Pilot the same published model in a lecture and pre-lab, on at least two device classes, with one physical or archived teaching run. Ask academics to configure it without an engineer. Record which controls they hide, whether the prediction survives handoff, time to compare data, and whether the learner can explain a held-out discrepancy. Compare a matched static worked example where feasible, collect baseline preparation and disclose small sample limitations. Pilot outcomes are evidence about usability and learning transfer; sales evidence requires separate conversations about procurement and support.
