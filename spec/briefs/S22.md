## B · Educational purpose

Drag a liquid composition and predict the equilibrium vapour. Distinguish composition at a given state from the amount of each phase.

The horizontal tie line ties together two different compositions that share one pressure.

Outcome: Recover liquid composition from specified vapour; optional given overall z and P derive phase fractions only inside the two-phase envelope. At pure endpoints both curves meet. Prerequisites: No additional block required. Apply / Analyse. Lecture demo, tutorial, guided or independent web activity. Practice link: Published equilibrium data after rights/mixture review. Industrial teaching context: Phase equilibrium and thermal utilities.

## C · Mathematical basis

Ideal binary at one fixed illustrative temperature, PsatA=2×10⁵ Pa, PsatB=10⁵ Pa; Pbubble=xA PA*+(1−xA) PB*; yA=xAPA*/Pbubble. Pdew=1/[yA/PA*+(1−yA)/PB*]. α=PA*/PB*. An x–y curve with constant α is a surrogate for a distillation exercise.

Independent quantities and engineering units appear below. Outputs are derived. Assume SI absolute state variables unless a quantity is explicitly a difference or a gauge reading. Algebraic variants use the listed boundary state and require no time initialisation.

## L · Learning activities

**Explore.** Drag a liquid composition and predict the equilibrium vapour. Distinguish composition at a given state from the amount of each phase.

**Investigate.** Commit a prediction before changing a control. At x=.5 predict whether the equilibrium vapour is half A. A is more volatile, so vapour is enriched to 2/3. Ask whether that means two-thirds of the feed is vapour.

**Challenge and assessment.** Recover liquid composition from specified vapour; optional given overall z and P derive phase fractions only inside the two-phase envelope. At pure endpoints both curves meet. Submit a result with units, one rejected interpretation and the condition that would change the answer. Extension: Use named binary mixture and T-dependent constituent properties plus activity coefficients, pressure corrections and data at stated T/P before quantitative real-mixture use.

## M · Verification and validation

## N · Software implementation

Evaluate bubble and dew branches at same fixed T. Lock the opposite composition. Analytic inverse ratio gives matched tie-line endpoints. Phase amount requires overall composition.

Compose reviewed kernels and scene primitives; use a small custom adapter for any model-specific geometry.
