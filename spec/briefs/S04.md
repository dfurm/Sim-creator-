## B · Educational purpose

Given a narrowing pipe that rises, predict the downstream tap reading. Toggle losses to discover which head is dissipated and which terms can be recovered.

Moving elevation or bore separately shows which head terms exchange and which losses dissipate mechanical energy.

Outcome: Choose pump head to recover a required outlet pressure; explain the difference between pressure recovery and energy dissipation. Introduce measured losses for a real rig. ## I · Assumptions and applicability

Steady incompressible single-phase flow, common streamline or section-average with declared factors; K is lumped. Ideal mode locks hp and K at zero. If calculated absolute pressure reaches vapour pressure suppress liquid prediction; absent temperature/vapour data flag unknown cavitation margin.

## L · Learning activities

**Explore.** Given a narrowing pipe that rises, predict the downstream tap reading. Toggle losses to discover which head is dissipated and which terms can be recovered.

**Investigate.** Commit a prediction before changing a control. Raise the outlet 1 m at fixed diameters. Both speed heads stay fixed; outlet pressure falls by ρg. Ask which measured taps would distinguish this from a loss.

**Challenge and assessment.** Choose pump head to recover a required outlet pressure; explain the difference between pressure recovery and energy dissipation. Introduce measured losses for a real rig. Submit a result with units, one rejected interpretation and the condition that would change the answer. ## M · Verification and validation

Default Δp=p₂−p₁=−1945.37 Pa (rounded); equal areas, elevations and zero losses give Δp=0. Adding 1 m head adds 9806.65 Pa.

Verify dimensions and either an independent analytical limit, conservation identity or alternative numerical solve. Reject non-finite inputs and out-of-domain combinations in I.
