# Learning design and curriculum progression

Start with a physical question whose answer could plausibly go two ways. Put the selected variable, diagram feature, graph marker and equation term in the same focus group. Introduce a second mechanism after a student can describe the first one. Present controls in pairs only when their mathematical roles are different and visible. A typical 15-minute investigation allocates 2 minutes to prediction, 4 to controlled changes, 4 to interpretation, 3 to assumption checking and 2 to retrieval in a new context. A lecture can use only the first three minutes.

| Mode | What the learner does | What is recorded | Example using the wall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explore | Change one physical quantity and compare to a pinned state | Optional bookmarked state and units | Drag thickness and conductivity separately, locate where slope changes. |
| Investigate | Commit direction and reason, vary one factor, interpret observed magnitude | Prediction before reveal, both states and explanation | Double first-layer thickness. Quantify the fraction of resistance it originally provided. |
| Challenge | Work to a target or diagnose an unfamiliar result, document exclusions | Design basis, candidates, validity and a claim with evidence | Meet a heat-flux ceiling under a thickness cap and explain a measured interface jump. |

Introductory preset exposes two controls and the diagram. Intermediate adds term highlighting, a shared plot and the assumptions panel. Advanced unlocks parameter dependence, fit, inverse problem and uncertainty, as separate author choices. An instructor can choose independent operation without overlays. Transfer checks change constraints, units or boundary conditions rather than just substituting new numbers. A knowledge check gives feedback tied to the mechanism and allows another attempt after an explanation. A student can skip motion and inspect the numerical table.

**Assessment rubric for an open design**. Award 0–2 each for a correct balance with units, physical interpretation, model applicability, feasible design/alternative and validation reasoning. A correct numeric answer alone earns at most the calculation component. Preserve ungraded prediction history.
